ॐ
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन । मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥
Transliteration

Karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana |
Ma karma-phala-hetur bhur ma te sango ’stvakarmani ||

Literal Translation
"You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself to be the cause of the results of your activities, nor be attached to inaction."
Deeper Philosophical Meaning
This timeless verse outlines the core philosophy of Karma Yoga (the path of unselfish action). It teaches us to shift our psychological focus away from anxiety about the future and anchor it entirely in the present moment.

When we constantly worry about the outcome ("the fruits"), our energy is divided, which increases stress and hinders performance. The verse provides three crucial life rules: focus completely on your immediate effort, accept that final outcomes are dictated by a vast network of factors beyond your solo control, and never give in to apathy or absolute inaction out of fear of failure. True freedom comes from doing your duty purely for the sake of excellence.
From the Shrimad Bhagavad Gita
